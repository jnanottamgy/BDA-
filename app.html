Scalable Analysis of Hourly Air Quality Data from Indian Monitoring Stations

Harshavardha Satish Kumar (1MS24CS065) · Guide: Uzma Sultana

Problem

CPCB stations record PM2.5, PM10, NO₂, SO₂, CO and O₃ every hour, producing millions of readings. The aim is to process them at scale and find when, where and how pollution changes.

Base paper

Bogomolov & Plotnikov (2024), Big Data technologies for atmospheric air pollution data, Computational Nanotechnology 11(1). It processes station data with PySpark and compares it with Pandas.

Our contribution

A reproducible pipeline on public Indian CPCB data, with documented cleaning rules and a defined benchmark, plus this interactive explorer.

Research questions

  1. Which months, seasons and hours have the highest PM2.5 and PM10?
  2. Which cities and stations are the most polluted?
  3. How are AQI categories distributed?
  4. How did the March–May 2020 lockdown change pollution?
  5. How complete is the sensor data?
  6. How does Spark-style processing compare with single-threaded Pandas?

Pipeline

CSV upload→Remove duplicates→Range checks → null→Join stations→Year / month / hour / season→SQL aggregates→Charts→AI insights

This page runs DuckDB-WASM, a columnar SQL engine, inside your browser. The same SQL runs in PySpark (src/spark_pipeline.py) for the course submission. No data leaves your computer, except the summary you choose to send to the AI.
